Inventory Decision Lab

How much service does inventory buy? Compare three replenishment policies against the same demand, supplier timing, and purchasing constraints.

Simulation, not achieved savings. All SKUs, demand, supplier terms, and costs are synthetic. Results are conditional on the displayed assumptions. This small-scenario walkthrough is not deployment approval; consult the separate held-out experiment.

What the model knows

  • 56 days of training history by default; decisions use only observations available through that day.
  • Unmet demand is observed and lost permanently, with no backorders or substitution.
  • Supplier lead times are known and deterministic. Arrivals occur before daily demand; orders are placed afterward.
  • Scarce budget goes first to SKUs with the lowest inventory-position coverage, using a frozen training rate.

What to validate next

  1. Measure actual lost demand, unit costs, supplier delays, and order minimums.
  2. Agree on a service target and a defensible shortage penalty.
  3. Run lead-time and budget stress scenarios; retain cases where every policy fails.
  4. Pilot prospectively before claiming operating savings.

Audit trail & assumptions

Ending inventory and paid in-transit orders remain assets with no terminal salvage credit. Acquisition spend is reported separately and excluded from the modeled operating-cost objective. Daily procurement cash caps include order fees; this is not a full cash-flow or profit model. Initial stock is endowed equally and not charged to the purchasing budget.
